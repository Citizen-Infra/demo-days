Who we are: CIBC

What CIBC is

The Citizen Infrastructure Builders Club. A collective of builders making civic and community infrastructure that people own and run themselves: local-first AI, deliberation tools, peer-knowledge systems, collaboration platforms.

What we stand for

Anti-extractive by default. You keep your data and your tools. Build it, share it, help others build their own. The give-back loop, not lock-in.

The goals for this day

  • Grow the collective. More builders in the room, in the Telegram, on the ecosystem map.
  • Get the tools used. Real downloads, installs, and use cases, not just views.
  • Make builders of the room. Someone leaves planning to present their own next time.

Who this one is for

This first demo day targets builders. Later sessions can target communities and funders.

In Megan's words

The goal really is to grow the collective. We want them to use our tools and to get them building their own tools.

We're targeting builders, so promote to your network so people will come.

Setting the stage · Kyle

What is an MCP?

From the May 5 planning sync

Why it leads

The room will not all be technical. A short, plain-language explainer keeps people from falling off before the demos. Cover what an AI model is, what Codex is, what Claude desktop is, and what an MCP actually is.

Kyle's own way of putting it

Why Megan wants it up front

For the intro we should do a very brief explainer into the concept of MCP. I do not want to lose people if they are not super technical.

Tie it to a follow-up

Float a "get your own MCP set up" session people can sign up for. That makes the intro a funnel, not just a lecture.

Act I · Kyle

Compass Suite

From the May 5 planning sync

What it is

MCPs used as scaffolds and workflows: local, internal plug-ins the AI calls to run scripts and batches as needed. Two compasses to show:

  • Critical Compass. Evaluates writing from an academic standpoint.
  • UX Heuristics Compass. Audits a site against over 107 checklist items, grades it on common usability standards, and outputs an HTML file with the grade, automated through Python.

Demo options on the table

  • Run Critical Compass on a dummy research report and show the HTML it produces.
  • Once a few orgs register, run a heuristic audit on one of their websites, live.
  • Pre-run the example, then show screenshots or short clips on a slide and narrate it, to avoid live latency and variability.

How to frame it

Push the use cases to the forefront. The two distinct ones (academic writing vs site auditing) are the hook.

Kyle's open question

I need to work on my presentation and what matters, and at the technical level, how technical I should get.

Megan's steer

The use cases, I think we should definitely push to the forefront, because you have a few different ones.

Goal

Three people say "I'd use this," plus one pilot conversation. End on a sign-up.

Act I · Artem

Book Power

From the May 5 planning sync

What it is

Turns books into skills and MCP servers that can be added as context to a project in Claude Code or Codex. For people using AI agents who also care about a specific knowledge domain: deliberation, commenting, product design.

What's already processed

Plurality, by Audrey Tang and Glen Weyl, via the book's GitHub repo. A few hours of work, about ten dollars to embed (the most expensive so far). EPUB books were cheaper to process.

The hard demo constraint

No terminal on screen, it scares people who do not use a terminal. Claude cowork is the friendlier interface to demo from. Plan to run it on the Mac mini, since the laptop is Windows.

Demo options on the table

  • Scenario. Show a community benefit: run a citizen assembly or engage neighbors using the book's wisdom through cowork plus the MCP server.
  • Landing-page chat. A simple page wired to different MCP servers, so people just open a URL and chat with the AI. A way to demo MCP servers to anyone.
  • URL to report (Megan's idea). Host Book Power as a site: drop in a URL, it pulls out the concepts mentioned, and returns a small downloadable PNG report people can share.
  • Trivia (conditional). The group chats answers and Book Power acts as the judge. Only if the landing-page chat gets built.

Hosting

The servers are local only for now and need to go online. Someone on the call offered to help host them the right way.

Action item

Schedule a separate install call with Kyle, Megan, and anyone in the community. It doubles as a capture test run.

When you run on someone else's system, you find a lot more problems with the workflow.

In Artem's words

I built Book Power for people who are using AI agents in their work, but also who are interested in specific knowledge domains.

Act II · Simone

Deliberation mapping

From the May 5 planning sync

Which tool

The deliberation-mapping tool, not DelibAI. DelibAI is not published yet and moves to a future demo day.

What it does

Search a topic in the search bar and find the assemblies that deliberated it. Click an assembly to see its contributions; click a contribution to see the metadata and, at the bottom, a representation of the deliberation. A topics view shows the most-spoken topics. It is an analytical tool for tracking democratic processes across many fora.

Be honest about the limits

The data is biased: it only covers deliberations available online, on a limited server. Name it as not the ultimate research tool, yet.

Who it serves

People in city tech tracking fora, contributions, topics, and arguments. The easy win: find what people in different fora said about a topic they care about.

The ask: contributors and data

It is really a data repo. The goal is more contributors and making the data valuable and downloadable. Educating the room on the idea of a data commons fits here.

Where it goes next

The natural continuity is a centralized deliberation standard: a common data model where every institution can plug in. The next step is to make it dynamic.

Already built

The embedding and the topic search.

Act II · Momcilo

Collaboration under Constraint

From the May 5 planning sync

What it is

A platform to experiment with goals and constraint-based strategies to maximize positive outcomes. The emphasis is real-life application, not an AI demo.

How it works

No deliberation, no discussion. One person sets the rules, and that authority rotates algorithmically. The aim is to maximize doing and eliminate talking about doing: low quality expectation, just create the churn, and exercise the collaboration muscle.

Failure as art

A failure scenario is framed as self-deprecating art, a mix of real and AI-generated content.

Already in use: the magazine strategy

Someone sets the scope and member count, people join, an editor is auto-selected, a WYSIWYG editor produces the issue as a PDF or website, participants promote it on social media, and a merit system allocates points for compliance, not quality. A podcast strategy (asynchronous) is being built next.

Lineage

The Oulipo movement of the 1960s and 70s pioneered creative constraints, like writing a whole book without the letter E.

Demo

There is a built-in demo mode, already recorded.

How to frame it, and use-case demos

  • Frame it as a playful experiment; anti-fragility is the point.
  • Seed a live session whose only constraint is "go collaborate with someone, then log it."
  • The token-drought case: pick up a task, run your AI on it, contribute it back to the repo, distributing development and token usage.

In Momcilo's words

The aim is to maximize doing and eliminate talking about doing.

Exercise the muscle for collaboration, that we can surrender our egos and submit to the control of others when it is their turn.

Naming note: "cell" carried the wrong connotations; "quorum" came up as an alternative.
